Case study · bolt.eu public web · concept · October 2026

Answers before the app.

A redesign concept for Bolt's public pages: Home, Rides, Scooters and Car-sharing. It keeps bolt.eu's own look and page order, and adds one thing to each page: the answer to the question that brought the visitor there. How much, how long, is there one near me. Then the app opens with the trip already filled in.

In short

The problem

bolt.eu explains its products well but rarely lets people act. Eight pages use six different main buttons, every rider button ends in the same generic QR code, and no page estimates your own trip.

What I designed

Pages that look and read like bolt.eu, with one working answer each: a price before install, the nearest scooter, a car you can "unlock". Booking stays in the app, which opens with the trip filled in.

How I would ship it

Six A/B tests, smallest first, each with one primary metric and guardrails. WCAG AA by default, AAA where it matters, and a performance budget for the map.

01 · Problem

A brochure that sends you away to act.

I reviewed eight English pages on desktop and mobile, plus the header, menu and footer, against five lenses: path to first ride, content accuracy, consistency, navigation and accessibility signals.

6different main button labels across 8 product pages
2 of 8pages let a visitor do anything without the app (Food: order online; Business: contact us)
0pages estimate the visitor's own trip; Scooters shows a per-minute rate without the unlock fee
  1. F1

    Every rider button ends in the same QR code. "Get Bolt", "Rent a scooter" and "Rent an e-bike" open one modal that doesn't know the product, the city or the price, and offers no link to send to a phone.

    High
  2. F2

    Online booking is promised, not offered. The Rides FAQ says you can request a ride online in some regions; the page has no way to do it, and its app link points to "#".

    High
  3. F3

    The Bolt 7 promo opens the Bolt 6 story. "Learn more" on the new scooter leads to an article that never mentions it.

    High
  4. F4 · F5

    Products hide behind a menu icon, and "Register" means partners. The menu mixes riders with drivers, couriers and restaurants, repeats links and doesn't match the footer.

    Medium
  5. F6

    The scooter price leaves out the unlock fee. The card says "from €0.15 per minute"; the FAQ on the same page says the total also includes an unlock fee.

    Medium
  6. F9

    Home reads as a transport company. Every headline starts with moving (rides, scooters, cars); food appears only as a second button, "Get Bolt Food". A first-time visitor can leave without learning that Bolt also delivers.

    Medium
A signal, not evidence

To sharpen the brief I also ran an AI-simulated usability test of the live site with 20 synthetic personas. In it, two "visitors" could not tell what Bolt is from the homepage, and two left Rides because they could not see what their own trip would cost. These match F1, F2, F6 and F9, so I used them to set priorities, not as proof. They are the first things I would check with five real first-time visitors.

02 · Brand fit

My first version solved the problem and lost the brand.

Version 1 answered every question on a dark, app-like city map. It worked, but when I put it next to bolt.eu, it did not look like Bolt anymore. A public page has to be recognised in a second, so I audited Bolt's house style and rebuilt every page inside it.

03 · Idea

Plan on the web, finish in the app.

People who reach bolt.eu mostly don't have the app yet. The page's job is to answer their question well enough that installing feels like the obvious next step, without rebuilding the app in the browser.

01

Answer first

Each page has one block that answers the visitor's question: cost, time, the nearest vehicle, the rules.

02

Recognisably Bolt

Bolt's page order, photography, colours and words. The new parts use the same cards, buttons and spacing.

03

One web language

The same map card, search, price row, sticky sub-menu and handoff on every page, so the site feels like one product.

04

Respect the person

Reduced motion, keyboard paths, AA contrast, no personal data collected, and no driver positions on a public page.

Button naming, one rule for every page

WhereLabelWhy
Header, every pageGet the appAlways in the same place with the same words; opens the app on the page's product.
Hero, main actionThe product's verb: Get Bolt · Rent a scooter · Rent a carSays what you will do, as bolt.eu already does on Scooters and Drive.
After an answer… in the app: Continue in the app · Reserve in the app · Unlock in the appMakes clear the next step happens in the app, with the trip filled in.
Leaving bolt.eu's productGet Bolt Food ↗The arrow and its accessible name say it opens another site.

04 · Pages

One question per page, answered where it comes up.

Every prototype page has Design notes in the top bar: numbered pins that explain, in place, why bolt.eu does it this way, what I kept, what I changed, how to build it and how to test it.

Home

"What is Bolt, and which way is best for me?"

  • Bolt's own homepage order: hero, six services, Bolt 7, about, earn, download, news.
  • "Get around. Get delivered." names both halves of Bolt in the first line (F9).
  • The race compares Ride, Scooter and Car-sharing on real routes. After it: "Staying in instead? Try Bolt Food", with a delivery time for the same place.

AHA: for 2 km the scooter wins, for the airport the ride does, and if you would rather stay in, food is 25 minutes away. Three products in one answer.

Open Home →
Rides

"How much, and how long?"

  • "Your price, before you install": the route follows real streets and one-way rules, with a fare range per ride type and the arrival time.
  • "From Tallinn Airport, priced in advance": five common trips with their fares. One click puts the trip into the planner.
  • One main action, "Continue in the app", carries the route, ride type and price (F1, F2).

AHA: the answer to one of the most searched questions, "bolt price to the airport", is on the page, before any install.

Open Rides →
Scooters & e-bikes

"Where can I ride and park, and what will it cost?"

  • "How to rent" becomes the map itself: step one finds the nearest scooter, its battery and the walk.
  • The full price includes the unlock fee (F6), and a battery check for this exact trip.
  • The Bolt 7 block plays a ride on the handlebar display: speed limit, the Old Town slow zone, green parking (F3).

AHA: "the scooter slows down by itself in the Old Town" is understood in seconds, not read in an FAQ.

Car-sharing

"Is there a car near me, and should I pay by the minute or the day?"

  • Hold to unlock: the hero turns "your phone is the key" into a gesture, keyboard included. Both car states are real studio frames, not CSS glows.
  • The unlocked car is a real car on the map below. "Show it on the map" scrolls to it, with fuel, range and the walk.
  • A planner compares minute and day rates for common trips and names the cheaper one.

AHA: the page shows a real car one minute away instead of asking for an install just to look.

Campaign page · Bolt 7

"What is new, and why should I care?"

  • On bolt.eu the Bolt 7 promo's "Learn more" opens the Bolt 6 story (F3). A launch deserves its own page.
  • Built only from the site's parts: dark hero, video block with pause, fact cards, final CTA. The one new piece, a scroll story, reuses the handlebar display from Scooters.
  • Facts come from Bolt's announcement and press, with sources on the page. "Not in your city yet?" sends Tallinn riders to today's scooters.

AHA: four AI and display features become one ride you scroll through: route, slow zone, pavement, parking.

On a phone

One column, one thumb.

  • Photos sit between the headline and the text, so the first screen shows what the page is about.
  • On maps, one finger scrolls the page and two fingers move the map, so the map never traps the scroll.
  • The handoff opens the app directly, no QR code. The footer folds into an accordion.

05 · What I cut

Six things I built, then took out.

Each one was impressive. Each one either copied the app, set an expectation the page couldn't meet, or worked against Bolt's own look.

Cut

A night city as the homepage hero

WhyIt was the strongest demo of v1, but it made the first screen look like an app, not like bolt.eu. Visitors first need to know what Bolt is.

KeptThe race, lower on the page, in day colours.

Cut

A dark theme across the site

Whybolt.eu is light. Black stays only where Bolt already uses it.

KeptDark Drive hero, Bolt 7, download band, footer.

Cut

A full ride replay with a named driver, pickup code and turn-by-turn

WhyIt rebuilt the app's in-trip screen on a page where nobody can book, so the end of the demo felt like a dead end.

KeptRoute, price, arrival time.

Cut

Live driver positions near the pickup

WhyA public page should never show where drivers are. Pickup time is an estimate by area.

Kept"Pickup about 2 min".

Cut

Real reserving and unlocking on the web

WhyThey belong to the app. The hero unlock is a demonstration; its next step says "continue in the app".

KeptCars, fuel, the walk, and a handoff on the chosen car.

Cut

Light effects drawn with CSS on a small photo

WhyThe car looked blurry and the glow looked fake, which hurt trust in the whole page.

KeptTwo matched 2K studio frames: lights off and lights on.

06 · Accessibility

AA by default, AAA where it matters.

Checked with automated tests (contrast, target size, reflow at 320 px) and manual keyboard passes. Not yet tested with screen-reader users.

WCAG 2.2LevelWhat the design does
1.4.3 ContrastAABody text 18:1, secondary text 5.6:1. Bolt green #2B8659 is only 4.3:1 as text on white, so text links use a darker #23744C (5.4:1). White text on green buttons keeps 4.5:1.
1.4.6 Contrast (enhanced)AAA, partlyBody text on white and on the dark bands passes 7:1. Secondary text stays at 5.6:1 on purpose, to keep the hierarchy readable.
1.1.1 Non-text contentAPhotos that add something get alt text; photos that only repeat their card's text, and the car's light layers, are marked decorative so a screen reader does not read the same thing twice. bolt.eu has no alt on most images (F8).
2.4.9 Link purpose (link only)AAARepeated labels like "Learn more", "View all" and the two Drive plan buttons have unique accessible names, so a list of links still makes sense out of context.
2.1.1 KeyboardAPlace search is a combobox with arrow keys and Enter. Hold to unlock also works with Enter. Photo rails scroll with arrow buttons. Footer groups are native disclosure buttons.
2.2.2 Pause, stop, hideAThe Bolt 7 video has a pause button. The race and the scooter ride can be skipped. Moving parts stop when they leave the screen.
2.3.3 Animation from interactionsAAAWith "reduce motion" on, the race shows its result straight away, the headlight beams and flicker are off, and the unlocked car simply appears lit. The hold still needs a hold, so the meaning stays.
2.5.8 Target sizeAAAll controls are at least 24 px; main actions are 44–52 px, which also meets AAA 2.5.5.
1.4.10 ReflowAAEvery page works at 320 px wide with no horizontal page scroll.
4.1.2 Name, role, valueAARIA combobox and listbox, pressed states on toggles and tabs, and one short status message per result (route, price, "unlocked"), never a stream of updates.

08 · Performance and search

Text first, map second.

Public pages are opened on cheap Android phones in 50 countries, often from search. The prototype paints text and the first photo first, loads the rest of the photos lazily, and loads the map data after; the data file is shared and cached across pages.

≈0.7–1.1 sLargest Contentful Paint, lab, throttled phone (1.6 Mbps, 4× slower CPU), across the five pages
≈5 suntil the live map is ready on the same profile; the page is usable before that
66–115 KBof HTML per page, photos in WebP; the 425 KB map file is loaded once for all pages

For production

Text and links rendered on the server for search. The map from Bolt's own map and routing stack as vector tiles, loaded when the visitor interacts or when the page is idle. Responsive images per breakpoint. A budget per page: LCP under 2.5 s and INP under 200 ms at p75 on a mid-range Android.

For search

Each product page answers a high-intent query in its own text ("bolt price to Tallinn airport", "how to rent a Bolt scooter in Tallinn", "rent a car in Tallinn"). Popular routes could get their own pages with the same components, a test for the SEO team.

Lab numbers from my own test runs, not field data.

09 · Shipping

Not a big-bang redesign: six tests, smallest first.

Every piece can ship on its own into today's bolt.eu, because it uses Bolt's own layout. The order follows effort against expected impact; each test has one primary metric and guardrails, so a win on one number can't hide a loss on another.

10 · Working with AI

Faster, with the standard kept high.

Where AI helped

Claude was my second reviewer for the audit, my pair programmer for the coded prototype, and a critic that pushed back on ideas, like the ride replay and the dark theme. AI-simulated personas sharpened the hypotheses. Higgsfield turned Bolt's own 926 px car photo into two matched 2K studio frames (lights off, lights on) for the Drive hero, and made the illustrations where Bolt had no photo. When standard accessibility tools were not available, I wrote a small checker with Claude; it caught the AA failure on the green band.

What I owned

The questions each page answers, the brand audit and the decision to rebuild v1, every cut in section 05, and the line between what the web should do and what belongs in the app. I checked every generated image against the original (livery, plate, wheels) and labelled it. Every number on the page is marked as real (distances, street names) or as an example (prices, wait times).
