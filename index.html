Case study · bolt.eu public web · concept · October 2026

Answers before the app.

A redesign concept for Bolt's public pages: Home, Rides, Scooters and Car-sharing. Each page answers the question that brought the visitor there (how much, how long, is there one near me) on a real map of Tallinn, then hands the trip to the app, ready to confirm.

In short

The problem

bolt.eu explains its products well but rarely lets people act. Eight pages use six different main buttons, every rider button ends in the same generic QR code, and no page estimates your own trip.

What I designed

Pages that answer first: price, time, the nearest vehicle and the rules, on real streets. Booking stays in the app, which opens with the trip already filled in.

How I would ship it

Five A/B tests, smallest first, each with one primary metric and guardrails. WCAG AA by default, AAA where it matters, and a performance budget for the map.

01 · Problem

A brochure that sends you away to act.

I reviewed eight English pages on desktop and mobile, plus the header, menu and footer, against five lenses: path to first ride, content accuracy, consistency, navigation and accessibility signals.

6different main button labels across 8 product pages
2 of 8pages let a visitor do anything without the app (Food: order online; Business: contact us)
0pages estimate the visitor's own trip; Scooters shows a per-minute rate without the unlock fee
  1. F1

    Every rider button ends in the same QR code. "Get Bolt", "Rent a scooter" and "Rent an e-bike" open one modal that doesn't know the product, the city or the price, and offers no link to send to a phone.

    High
  2. F2

    Online booking is promised, not offered. The Rides FAQ says you can request a ride online in some regions; the page has no way to do it, and its app link points to "#".

    High
  3. F3

    The Bolt 7 promo opens the Bolt 6 story. "Learn more" on the new scooter leads to an article that never mentions it.

    High
  4. F4 · F5

    Products hide behind a menu icon, and "Register" means partners. The menu mixes riders with drivers, couriers and restaurants, repeats links and doesn't match the footer.

    Medium
  5. F6

    The scooter price leaves out the unlock fee. The card says "from €0.15 per minute"; the FAQ on the same page says the total also includes an unlock fee.

    Medium
A signal, not evidence

To sharpen the brief I also ran an AI-simulated usability test of the live site with 20 synthetic personas. In it, two "visitors" could not tell what Bolt is from the homepage, and two left Rides because they could not see what their own trip would cost. These match F1, F2 and F6, so I used them to set priorities, not as proof. They are the first things I would check with five real first-time visitors.

02 · Idea

Plan on the web, finish in the app.

People who reach bolt.eu mostly don't have the app yet. The page's job is to answer their question well enough that installing feels like the obvious next step, without rebuilding the app in the browser.

01

Answer first

Every page opens on the visitor's question, not on a slogan: cost, time, the nearest vehicle, the rules.

02

Real, not illustrative

Real streets, one-way rules and street names make the numbers believable. Example prices are labelled as examples.

03

One web language

The same map, search, panel, markers, price row and handoff on every page, so the site feels like one product.

04

Respect the person

Reduced motion, keyboard paths, AA contrast, no personal data collected, and no driver positions on a public page.

03 · Pages

One question per page, answered on the map.

Rides

"How much, and how long?"

  • The route follows real streets and one-way rules. Fare range per ride type, pickup and arrival time.
  • "+5 min" and "+9 min" marks show where the time goes: slow in the Old Town, fast on Tartu mnt.
  • One main action, "Continue in the app", carries the route, ride type and price (F1, F2).

AHA: the answer to the most searched question ("bolt price to the airport") is on the page, before any install.

Open Rides →
Scooters & e-bikes

"Where can I ride and park, and what will it cost?"

  • Parked scooters with battery rings, green parking, the Old Town slow zone and no-parking areas.
  • Full price including the unlock fee (F6), and a battery check for this exact trip.
  • "What the scooter shows you": the handlebar display explains the three rules first-timers worry about, on the real route.

AHA: "the scooter slows down by itself in the Old Town" is understood in seconds, not read in an FAQ.

Open Scooters →
Car-sharing

"Is there a car near me, and should I pay by the minute or the day?"

  • Hold to unlock: the hero turns "your phone is the key" into a gesture, keyboard included.
  • Cars near you with fuel, range and the walk. Reserving opens the app on the chosen car.
  • A planner compares minutes and day rates for four common trips and names the cheaper one.

AHA: the page shows a real car two minutes away instead of asking for an install just to look.

Open Car-sharing →
Home

"What is Bolt, and which way is best for me?"

  • The first screen shows the city moving with every service, so "what is Bolt" is answered without a sentence.
  • The race compares Ride, Scooter and Car-sharing on their real routes. Fastest and cheapest are labelled; each lane opens its page with the trip filled in.
  • Day or night follows the real sun over Tallinn. A pause button stops the moving city.

AHA: for 2 km the scooter wins; for the airport the ride does. Bolt sells three products in one answer.

Open Home →
On a phone

Map on top, answer below, one thumb.

  • The map takes the top half; the panel follows. After a choice, the page scrolls to show the route and the price together.
  • One finger scrolls the page, two fingers move the map, so the map never traps the scroll.
  • On a phone the handoff opens the app directly, no QR code.

04 · What I cut

Five things I built, then took out.

Each one was impressive. Each one either copied the app, set an expectation the page couldn't meet, or worked against Bolt's own look.

Cut

A full ride replay with a named driver, pickup code and turn-by-turn

WhyIt rebuilt the app's in-trip screen on a page where nobody can book, so the end of the demo felt like a dead end.

KeptRoute, price, time marks.

Cut

Live driver positions near the pickup

WhyA public page should never show where drivers are. Pickup time is now an estimate by area.

Kept"Pickup about 2 min".

Cut

Reservation countdown and unlocking on the web

WhyReserving and unlocking belong to the app. Doing them on the page duplicated the product.

KeptCars, fuel, the walk, and an app handoff on the chosen car.

Cut

A dark theme across the site

Whybolt.eu is light. Black stays only where Bolt already uses it: the Drive hero, the footer and the safety band.

KeptNight on Home, only after real sunset.

Cut

An "I want to…" list as the homepage hero

WhyIn review it read as a second menu, not as an answer.

KeptThe living city and the race.

05 · Accessibility

AA by default, AAA where it matters.

Checked with automated tests (contrast, target size, reflow at 320 px) and manual keyboard passes. Not yet tested with screen-reader users.

WCAG 2.2LevelWhat the design does
1.4.3 ContrastAABody text 18:1, secondary text 5.6:1. Bolt green #2B8659 is only 4.3:1 as text on the page background, so text links use a darker #23744C (5.4:1). White on green buttons stays at 4.5:1.
1.4.6 Contrast (enhanced)AAA, partlyBody and night text pass 7:1. Secondary text stays at 5.6:1 on purpose, to keep the hierarchy readable.
2.1.1 KeyboardAPlace search is a combobox with arrow keys and Enter. Hold to unlock also works by holding Enter. Each map task has a keyboard path: search instead of tapping, a list of the nearest cars, the nearest scooter chosen for you.
2.2.2 Pause, stop, hideAThe moving city has a pause button. The route pulse stops after three runs. The scooter explainer can be paused.
2.3.3 Animation from interactionsAAAWith "reduce motion" on, camera flights become short cuts, the race shows its result straight away, and the route pulse is off. Hold to unlock still needs a hold, so the meaning stays.
2.5.8 Target sizeAAAll controls are at least 24 px; main actions are 44–52 px, which also meets AAA 2.5.5.
1.4.10 ReflowAAEvery page works at 320 px wide with no horizontal page scroll.
4.1.2 Name, role, valueAARIA combobox and listbox, pressed states on toggles, and one short status message per result (route, price, winner), never a stream of updates.

06 · Performance and search

Text first, map second.

Public pages are opened on cheap Android phones in 50 countries, often from search. The prototype paints text and controls first and loads the map data after; the data file is shared and cached across pages.

≈0.5 sLargest Contentful Paint, lab, throttled phone (1.6 Mbps, 4× slower CPU)
≈3.3 suntil the live map is ready on the same profile; the text is usable before that
62–88 KBper page of HTML; the 425 KB map file is loaded once for all pages

For production

Text and links rendered on the server for search. The map from Bolt's own map and routing stack as vector tiles, loaded when the visitor interacts or when the page is idle. City data per page. A budget per page: LCP under 2.5 s and INP under 200 ms at p75 on a mid-range Android.

For search

Each product page answers a high-intent query in its own text ("bolt price to Tallinn airport", "where can I park a scooter in the Old Town"). Popular routes could get their own pages with the same components, a test for the SEO team.

Lab numbers from my own test runs, not field data.

07 · Shipping

Not a big-bang redesign: five tests, smallest first.

Every piece can ship on its own. The order follows effort against expected impact; each test has one primary metric and guardrails so a win on one number can't hide a loss on another.

1Handoff that knows the product (QR + send a link)A handoff that names the product and trip turns more desktop clicks into installs.App installs per CTA clickBounce rate, modal close rate

Beyond Tallinn

The city comes from the URL or location, with a picker. Where there is no vehicle data, the same components fall back to a static price table and the handoff. Text is translated per locale; prices come from the pricing service, never from the page.

08 · Working with AI

Faster, with the standard kept high.

Where AI helped

Claude was my second reviewer for the audit, my pair programmer for the coded prototype, and a critic that pushed back on ideas, like the ride replay and the dark theme. AI-simulated personas sharpened the hypotheses. Higgsfield made the generic car and scooter photos.

What I owned

The questions each page answers, the priorities, every cut in section 04, and the line between what the web should do and what belongs in the app. Every number on the page is labelled as real (distances, street names) or as an example (prices, wait times).
